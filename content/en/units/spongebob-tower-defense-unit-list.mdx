## How Units Work: Rarities, Roles, and Upgrade Priorities

Every entry on the SpongeBob Tower Defense unit list is a tower you place along a path. Enemies walk the route, your units shoot, and the cash you earn from kills and wave completions funds upgrades. That loop is simple on paper, but three details decide most matches.

- **Placement beats raw stats.** A mid-tier unit on a corner where the path doubles back will out-damage a stronger unit parked on a straightaway.
- **Upgrades compound.** Most units gain far more from reaching a high upgrade level than from simply having a high rarity at level one.
- **Rarity is a starting point, not a verdict.** Player experience consistently shows that a fully upgraded lower-rarity unit can outperform a base-level Mythic when it sits in the right lane.

### Habits That Actually Win Waves

- **Buy economy first.** Farm units pay for everything else. Skipping them is the most common reason runs collapse in the mid-game.
- **Upgrade tall, not wide.** Two units at max level usually beat six units at level two.
- **Reposition between waves.** If a lane is leaking, moving one unit is cheaper than upgrading three.
- **Match damage type to the wave.** Swarm waves reward splash damage; boss waves reward single-target burst.
- **Scout the path before you place.** Long stretches where the route doubles back are the best real estate on any map.
- **Keep one flex slot.** Patches shift the meta, and a flexible slot lets you adapt without rebuilding your whole board.

## Values, Tier Lists, and Trading Without Getting Scammed

Because units can be traded, an entire economy has grown around the SpongeBob Tower Defense unit list. Community value lists rank units by what players are willing to pay, while community tier lists rank them by combat performance. Those are two different questions, and mixing them up is how players lose good units in bad trades.

A few ground rules drawn from player experience:

- **Values move fast.** A unit that was top-tier last update can slide down the rankings after a balance patch.
- **Never trade first with a stranger.** Use a trusted middleman or a reputable trading hub.
- **Screenshot everything.** Trades go wrong, and having proof matters.
- **Cross-check at least two sources.** One list can be outdated; three lists agreeing is a real signal.
